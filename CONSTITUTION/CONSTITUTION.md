# CreatorOS Constitution

## 1. Purpose

This Constitution protects the long-term integrity of CreatorOS. It defines what cannot be compromised, regardless of circumstance, pressure, or convenience.

Where the Manifesto explains why CreatorOS exists and Principles guide how engineering decisions are made, the Constitution defines the boundaries within which both purpose and principles must operate. It does not describe intent or preference — it defines constraint.

Nothing in CreatorOS, at any level, may intentionally contradict this document. Where a lower-authority document is found to conflict with a constitutional article, the conflict is resolved in favor of the article, and the conflicting document is corrected.

This Constitution is deliberately narrow. It does not attempt to anticipate every future decision CreatorOS will require; it exists only to fix the boundaries within which those decisions are made. Anything not addressed here is left to architecture, principles, and governance to determine — but nothing determined at those levels may cross the boundaries fixed here.

---

## 2. Architectural Supremacy

Architecture always precedes implementation. No implementation may exist without an architectural decision that authorizes it.

Implementation must conform to architecture. Where an implementation cannot satisfy the architecture it depends on, the implementation is deficient — the architecture does not yield to accommodate it.

Implementation must never redefine architecture. A workaround, shortcut, or convenient reinterpretation discovered during implementation does not become architecture merely by being convenient or by working.

Architectural changes require explicit approval. No architectural constraint is altered by informal consensus, precedent, or accumulated practice. It is altered only through the deliberate process established for that purpose.

This ordering is not procedural formality. It exists because architecture, once compromised by implementation convenience, does not visibly break — it quietly erodes, decision by unrecorded decision, until the system no longer resembles what was intended. Architectural supremacy is the constitutional defense against that erosion.

---

## 3. Creator Primacy

The creator is the primary entity of CreatorOS. Every subsystem, process, and decision exists in service of the creator, not the reverse.

All systems exist to preserve and support creator continuity. A system that does not serve this purpose, however useful it may be for some other end, has no claim to a place within CreatorOS.

No subsystem may become more important than the creator it serves. Where a subsystem's requirements conflict with the creator's continuity or interests, the subsystem must yield.

This article applies regardless of how central a subsystem becomes over time. Utility, adoption, or operational importance do not grant a subsystem standing to override the creator it was built to serve.

---

## 4. Identity Continuity

A creator's identity is continuous. It is not reset, fragmented, or reconstructed from scratch by the passage of time or by changes to the systems that support it.

Identity may evolve intentionally. Deliberate, chosen change is compatible with continuity and is expected over time.

Identity must never drift accidentally because of implementation. A change in identity that occurs as a side effect of technical decisions, rather than as a deliberate act, is a violation of this article, regardless of its scale.

Continuity has higher priority than convenience. No implementation convenience justifies a compromise to identity continuity.

A distinction must always be preserved between change that the creator has chosen and change that has merely happened to the creator as a consequence of some technical decision made elsewhere. Only the former is compatible with this article.

---

## 5. Memory Integrity

Recorded memory is authoritative. Once memory has been deliberately recorded, it is treated as a true record of what occurred, not as a suggestion open to casual revision.

Memory must not be silently rewritten, discarded, or fabricated. Any alteration to recorded memory that occurs without explicit acknowledgment is a violation of this article, regardless of the intention behind it.

Changes to persistent knowledge must be explicit. Where memory must be corrected, superseded, or removed, that action must be a recorded, deliberate act — never an invisible byproduct of some other process.

Memory integrity does not require that memory be permanent or unchangeable. It requires that every change be visible, attributable, and deliberate, so that the record of what a creator has known and experienced can itself be trusted.

---

## 6. Explicit Architecture

Architectural decisions must be documented. A decision that exists only in practice, discussion, or the memory of those who made it is not yet architecture.

Undocumented architecture is not architecture. If a rule governing CreatorOS cannot be pointed to in recorded form, it does not bind CreatorOS and must not be treated as though it does.

Every accepted architectural decision must have a canonical source. Where a decision is referenced, that reference must resolve to one authoritative document, not to memory, convention, or inference.

This article treats documentation as constitutive rather than descriptive. A decision does not exist because someone recalls making it; it exists because it has been written down and given a place among the recorded architecture of CreatorOS.

---

## 7. Technology Independence

CreatorOS is independent of specific technologies. Its architecture is defined in terms that do not depend on any particular tool, language, platform, or method to remain valid.

No implementation, vendor, framework, or model defines CreatorOS. Whatever technology is used at a given time is a means of realizing CreatorOS, not a component of what CreatorOS fundamentally is.

Technology is replaceable. Any implementation, however central it currently seems, can be replaced without altering what CreatorOS is.

Purpose is not. The purpose CreatorOS serves does not change because the means of serving it change.

This independence must be actively preserved, not merely assumed. Architecture that can only be described in terms of a specific tool or method has already violated this article, whether or not that tool or method is named.

---

## 8. Governance

Changes to foundational documents require Architecture Board approval. The Manifesto, this Constitution, and the Principles may be amended only through the Architecture Board's deliberate process, never through informal accumulation of changes.

Operational contributors implement architecture but do not redefine it. A contributor's role, however extensive its responsibilities, does not include the authority to alter what has been architecturally decided.

Governance exists to preserve continuity rather than control contributors. Its purpose is not to constrain people for its own sake, but to ensure that CreatorOS remains coherent and continuous across time, contributors, and circumstances.

Where governance and expedience conflict, governance prevails. A decision made outside the established process does not gain legitimacy from having produced a good outcome.

---

## 9. Consistency

Terminology must remain consistent. A term, once defined, retains that definition everywhere it is used within CreatorOS.

Each architectural concept has exactly one canonical definition. Where a concept appears to require a second definition, the correct response is to reconcile it with the first, not to allow both to stand.

Conflicting definitions must be resolved explicitly. A conflict between definitions is never left unresolved, and it is never resolved silently in favor of whichever definition is more convenient at the time.

Duplication is discouraged. Restating a concept in new words, rather than referencing its canonical definition, invites drift and must be avoided.

Consistency is not pursued for aesthetic reasons. Divergent terminology for the same concept is an early symptom of divergent understanding, and divergent understanding precedes architectural drift.

---

## 10. Evolution

CreatorOS is expected to evolve. This Constitution does not assume a static system; it assumes one that changes deliberately over a long period of time.

Evolution must be intentional. Change that occurs without a clear, deliberate decision behind it is not evolution — it is drift, and drift is what this Constitution exists in part to prevent.

Evolution occurs through documented architectural decisions. Every change of consequence to CreatorOS's architecture must be traceable to a specific, recorded decision.

Unrecorded evolution is architectural drift. Where change has occurred but no record of a decision exists, that change has no standing and must be examined, not assumed to be valid.

The distinction between evolution and drift is not a matter of degree. A large, deliberate, well-documented change is evolution. A small, unrecorded change is drift, regardless of how minor it appears.

---

## Final Articles

- Architecture before implementation.
- Continuity before convenience.
- Identity before technology.
- Memory before inference.
- Explicit decisions before assumptions.
- Documentation before implementation.
